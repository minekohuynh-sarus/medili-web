From farm to cup

Farm to cup is a way of making coffee so that drinkers know which farm their cup comes from and whose hands it has passed through. This page shows the stages a cup of coffee goes through and, at today's coffee price, how much the grower receives from a cup.

  2. Di LinhTraders, dealersCollect from many households, weigh and pay
  3. Di LinhMill, exporterSieve, grade, polish, bag
  4. Di LinhRoasterRoast, pack, brand
  5. Di LinhCaféBrews and serves
  6. Di LinhDrinkerPays for a cup

  6. Di LinhDrinkerReads the lot number and roast date on the bag tag

The grower's share is revenue, before fertilizer, picking labor and irrigation. Losses in MeDili's model: 5% defective beans removed, 16% lost in roasting, and 100 kg of ripe cherries give 18 kg of green coffee.

What farm to cup means

Farm to cup is a way of working, not a certification. There are four signs to recognize it.

Knowing the farm, knowing the lot

Each bag of coffee states the farm, lot code and roast date. Drinkers know where their cup comes from.

Fewer middlemen

Every change of hands adds to the price. With fewer middlemen, more of the value stays close to where the coffee is grown.

Prices by quality

Ripe cherries and careful processing are sold on the quality of the lot, rather than just the daily green coffee price.

Roasted close to drinking day

Roasted coffee needs to rest for a few days, then tastes best over the next few weeks. A short chain gets coffee to drinkers sooner.

About 80% of Vietnam's coffee output is still sold as green coffee, mostly Robusta. So most of the value from roasting, packing and brewing lies outside the growing areas.

A 35,000 VND cup: how much does the grower receive

The calculation below uses the Lâm Đồng green coffee price on the morning of Nov 7, 2026 and the loss model on MeDili's Fermentation with Brewer's Yeast page.

  1. 1

    A cup of phin uses 25 g of roasted coffee.

  2. 2

    25 g of roasted coffee needs about 31.3 g of green coffee, because defective beans are removed and water is lost in roasting. That green coffee comes from about 174 g of ripe cherries.

  3. 3

    At 83,600 VND/kg, 31.3 g of green coffee is worth about 2,619 VND. The café buys 25 g of roasted coffee at MeDili's wholesale price for about 6,300 VND.

  4. 4

    For a cup selling at 35,000 VND, the grower's share is about 7.5%. One kilogram of green coffee makes about 32 cups, which sell at the café for about 1.1 million VND.

The rest pays for many things. The café pays rent, staff, milk, sugar, ice and cups. The roaster pays for processing, roasting, packaging and transport. Farm to cup changes who does those stages. At MeDili, the farm, the processing facility and the roastery belong to the same house, all in Di Linh, so the value of processing and roasting stays in the growing area.

The grower's share here is revenue, before costs. The monthly costs of fertilizer, picking labor and irrigation are on the The Farm in 12 Months page.
