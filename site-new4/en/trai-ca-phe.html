Anatomy of the Origin Robusta Cherry

A ripe coffee cherry has five layers around the bean, plus the stem that joins it to the branch. Tap each part of the picture to see what it is and at which step it is removed. Change the processing method in Figure 2 to see what is left from 100 kg of ripe cherries.

Figure 1 Longitudinal section of a ripe Robusta cherry
Figure 2 What's left of 100 kg of ripe cherries

100 kg of ripe cherries leaves about 18 kg of green coffee and 14.4 kg of roasted coffee, enough for about 57 packs of 250 g. All three methods give the same amount of green coffee; they differ in which layer is removed before drying.

Seven parts, from outside in

Skin, pulp, mucilage, parchment and silverskin are the five layers around the bean. Together with the stem, they make up the seven numbered parts in Figure 1.

  1. 1

    Stem pedicel

    Coffee cherries grow in clusters close to the branch nodes, each held by a very short stem. One cluster usually has ripe, half-ripe and green cherries, so good picking means taking ripe cherries one by one. Stripping the whole cluster mixes green cherries into the batch, and the cup turns astringent and pungent.

    At MeDili

    The MeDili farm calendar splits the harvest into 3 rounds, from November to early January, with at most 5% green cherries per batch.

  2. 2

    Skin exocarp

    The outermost layer is thin and tough, turning from green to yellow and then red as it ripens. The skin holds water in the pulp and keeps out insects and mold. When whole cherries are dried, the skin dries into a dark, brittle layer that seals in the bean.

    At MeDili

    Skin color is the first gauge for choosing cherries. At the float-sorting station, floaters rise to the surface and are skimmed off.

  3. 3

    Pulp mesocarp

    Just under the skin is the sweet, juicy flesh. Skin and pulp make up about 40% of the fresh cherry's weight. In MeDili's model, 95 kg of sorted cherries becomes 57 kg after pulping. The pulper squeezes the cherries through a narrow gap; the skin and pulp burst off, and the beans, still coated in mucilage, slip through.

    At MeDili

    After pulping, the skin and pulp go to the compost shed and become organic fertilizer for the farm.

  4. 4

    Mucilage pectin layer

    Clinging to the parchment is a clear, slippery gel rich in pectin and sugar. Water can't wash this layer off. In the fermentation tank, yeasts and microbes eat the sugar and break down the pectin, producing acids and many aroma compounds. How much mucilage is kept is what separates Washed, Honey and Natural.

    At MeDili

    The Premium line is fermented Honey or Washed style to bring out fruit aromas. The Classic line is Natural-processed Robusta.

  5. 5

    Parchment endocarp

    Each bean sits in a hard, paper-thin, straw-colored shell. This layer keeps the bean intact during drying and storage. Beans still in this shell are called parchment coffee. During hulling, the parchment cracks and is blown away.

    At MeDili

    The aspirator before the roaster sucks the last bits of parchment and dust into the cyclone dust separator.

  6. 6

    Silverskin spermoderm

    Right against the bean is a very thin silvery film that even tucks into the center cut. A little comes off during hulling or polishing; most goes into the roaster with the bean. As the bean expands in the drum, the silverskin flakes off as light chaff.

    At MeDili

    The roaster's fan pulls smoke and chaff into the cyclone dust separator, keeping the batch clean.

  7. 7

    Bean endosperm

    The core is the seed, hard and gray-yellow when raw. Usually each cherry holds two beans lying flat side to flat side, each flat side with a groove down the middle. Green coffee contains sugars, acids, proteins and caffeine. The heat of roasting turns them into hundreds of compounds that make up the flavor in the cup.

    At MeDili

    Roasted at the Di Linh roastery, in 8 levels from Cinnamon to Italian.

Which layers make Natural, Honey and Washed different

All three start from the same ripe cherry. They differ in which layers still cling to the bean when it goes out to dry. Dashed lines show what has been removed.

At MeDili, the Classic line is Natural-processed Robusta. The Premium line is fermented Honey or Washed style to bring out fruit aromas. See each station in Fermentation with Brewer's Yeast and Co-fermentation with Fruit.

Two beans or one

Usually each cherry nurtures two beans lying flat side to flat side, which is why a coffee bean has one flat side with a groove down the middle. About 5–10% of cherries nurture only one bean. That bean grows to fill the whole cavity, evenly round, and is called culi coffee.

Round beans tumble in the roasting drum differently from flat ones, so culi is usually screened out and roasted as a separate batch. MeDili selects culi separately, lot by lot.
