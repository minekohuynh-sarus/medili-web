Aroma groups by origin at this roast level

Faded cells on the wheel are aromas that barely stand out at the selected roast level. Defect aromas stay unchanged because they do not depend on roast level.

How to read
  1. Smell, taste, then choose a group on the inner ring: sweet, fruity, nutty…
  2. Move out to the middle ring if you can tell more: cocoa or roasted nuts?
  3. Name an outer-ring aroma only when you are sure. If unsure, stopping at the inner ring is also right.
  4. Defect aromas sit at the bottom of the wheel. If you find one here, trace the fault back to the farm, fermentation tank, drying yard or warehouse.

Four origins of aroma

Where the aromas in your cup come from.

Coffee tasters often group aromas by when they form. This grouping is for nose training, not exact chemistry: one aroma can come from several pathways.

Enzymatic

Formed as the cherry grows and ripens, already present in the bean. Light and quick to fade, most evident in light roasts.

Examples: Potato, pea, cucumber, tea rose, coffee blossom, lemon, apricot, apple, honey.

Sugar caramelization

Sugars and amino acids react during roasting, most strongly at a medium roast: Maillard reactions and caramelization.

Examples: Vanilla, butter, toast, caramel, dark chocolate, roasted almond, roasted peanut, roasted hazelnut, walnut.

Dry distillation

Fiber and chlorogenic acids heated for a long time, prominent in dark roasts: wood, spice, smoke.

Examples: Cedar, clove, pepper, coriander seed, malt, pipe tobacco, roasted coffee.

Defect aromas

Not produced by good beans but by spoiled cherries, over-fermentation, dampness and mold, or old beans. They can appear at any roast level.

Examples: Earthy, straw, fruit skin and pulp, leather, stewed meat, cigarette smoke, medicinal, rubber.

Train your nose in four weeks

  
Your nose gets sharper by smelling real things and keeping regular notes; you don't need an expensive aroma kit to start.

  
  1. Week 1: smell the real thing first
    Pick 6 aromas from your kitchen: lemon, orange, apple, honey, roasted peanuts, cocoa. Smell each for 10 seconds with your eyes closed and say its name out loud. Write one sentence describing how it feels.
  2. Week 2: match them in the cup
    Brew 2 different cups, for example Robusta Classic and Arabica Premium. Smell the dry grounds, then smell each cup hot and again as it cools. Look for the 6 aromas from week 1.
  3. Week 3: work from the inside out
    Each time you taste, pick a group on the inner ring first (sweet, fruity, nutty…), then look for the middle group and the specific aroma. If unsure, stop at the inner ring.
  4. Week 4: log and compare
    Use the tasting sheet below for every cup. After a month, compare your sheets to see which groups your nose is sensitive to and which are still weak.
