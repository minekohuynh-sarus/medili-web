The Coffee Identification Machine

Eight coffee samples pass through eight stations: farm, ripe cherries, bean inspection table, sieve, scale, laboratory, tasting table and decision unit. Looking and tasting are qualitative; sieving, weighing and analysis are quantitative. The focus is Origin Robusta Di Linh: how to recognize it, and how it differs from high-yield Robusta.

Focus · Origin Robusta Di Linh

Origin Robusta (Cà Sẻ) is a variety, not a bean shape or a grade.

“Sẻ” is a folk name, not an official classification; the general name is still Robusta. Cà Sẻ, Robusta Sẻ, Ro Sẻ and small-bean Robusta all refer to the old Robusta variety (Origin Robusta) that the French brought to Vietnam in the early 20th century and took up to Di Linh and across the Central Highlands to plant, before the selected varieties of the WASI institute existed. The trees are propagated from seed, so the population is uneven: each tree has its own shape, cherries and yield. The name reflects the small beans, small cherries and compact canopy.

An analysis of 126 samples in WASI's gene bank showed that most Vietnamese Robusta material belongs to the ER genetic group, from the Congo River basin. From 1976, WASI began breeding on a large scale; the lines TR4, TR9, TR11, then TR14 and TR15 gradually replaced Origin Robusta thanks to their high yields and large beans. Origin Robusta is rarely planted now, but it is the foundation material of Vietnamese Robusta and holds many genes adapted to drought, pests and diseases.

MeDili's Origin Robusta farm

  • The Di Linh plateau sits at about 1,000 m; the Robusta grows at about 900–1,000 m.
  • Farmed since 2010; the trees are 15 years old and cared for naturally.
  • The cool climate ripens the cherries slowly: they are picked in several passes from November to early January, with at most 5% green cherries.
  • MeDili's observation: old Origin Robusta trees give a delicious cup, rich in caffeine.

Seven signs of Origin Robusta

  1. No graft scar at the baseThe trees grow from seed. Walk a row of 20 trees and you'll see different shapes, leaves and cherries.Farm · qualitative
  2. Small cherries, dense clustersCherries smaller than grafted lines; color and ripeness vary from tree to tree.Cherry · qualitative
  3. Small, dense beans with thick backsShort and rounded, with a straight center cut like all Robusta.Bean · qualitative
  4. Light on the scaleAn estimated 13–17 g per 100 beans; the TR4, TR9 and TR11 lines grown in Lâm Đồng weigh 21.6–29.6 g.Weighing · quantitative
  5. Few large-screen beansMost fall between screens 13 and 16; grafted lines in Lâm Đồng have 87–99% retained on screen 16.Sieving · quantitative
  6. Strong, clearly bitter, long finishDark chocolate, cocoa; full body, low acidity. High-yield Robusta is bitter but lighter in flavor.Tasting · sensory
  7. Farm records existFarm code, planting year, seed source. This is the strongest evidence; for certainty, run a DNA test.Traceability

Which scale to score by

  • Robusta: the Fine Robusta scale, covering fragrance/aroma, flavor, aftertaste, salt/acid ratio, bitter/sweet ratio, mouthfeel, balance, uniformity, clean cup and overall. 80 points and above is Fine Robusta.
  • Arabica: the SCA scale. Catimor, Typica and Moka are all scored on it.
  • Liberica, Excelsa: no widely used dedicated scale yet; the Arabica form is usually borrowed.
  • To compare varieties: roast light to medium with the same roast profile, rest 8–24 hours, 5 cups per sample, taste blind. A dark roast hides the differences.

Common misconceptions

Six things that are easy to get wrong when selling coffee.

Origin Robusta is not Culi

Origin Robusta is a tree variety. Culi is a bean shape: the cherry develops a single round bean; it occurs in every variety, about 4–5% of a crop. There are Origin Robusta Culi, high-yield Culi and Arabica Culi.

Small beans aren't necessarily Origin Robusta

The TR4 line grown in Gia Lai is only 15.3 g per 100 beans, on a par with Origin Robusta. Drought, poor nutrition, picking unripe cherries or sieving out the small beans all make beans smaller.

Caffeine can't prove the variety

Robusta has nearly twice the caffeine of Arabica, but within one species caffeine varies with region, ripeness and year. No caffeine threshold separates Origin Robusta from high-yield Robusta.

“Moka” is not a general name for good Arabica

Moka Cầu Đất is a specific Arabica variety, often described as a dwarf form of Bourbon: very small, round beans, only about 1–2 kg per tree per year.

Green bean color doesn't reveal the variety

Color changes with processing, moisture and storage time. Read the bean shape and center cut instead of the color.

Culi is not “twice as strong”

Culi carries the composition of its parent tree. Round beans tumble evenly, so they roast more evenly and the cup may taste rounder, but it is not guaranteed.

Names on the market

Many trade names are variety names; many others are bean shapes, regions, grades or processing methods. Ask the right question and you'll buy the wrong thing less often.

Sample inspection procedure when buying green coffee

The ten steps MeDili uses when receiving a sample. Steps 1–6 are done at the roastery in one session; steps 7–8 need a sample roaster and a tasting table; step 9 only when certification is needed.

Step 1Take a sample

About 300 g, mixed from several bags. Record the lot code and the name it was offered under.

Step 2Measure moisture

At most 12.5% under TCVN 4193. With high moisture, every weight reading is wrong.

Step 3Inspect 100 beans

Turn them flat side up and count straight cuts, S-cuts, wrinkled off-center cuts and round beans.

Step 4Sieve

Screens 18 (7.10 mm), 16 (6.30 mm), 13 (5.00 mm). Weigh each layer.

Step 5Weigh 100 beans

Whole, unbroken beans. Weigh 3 times and take the average.

Step 6Count defects

Black, brown, immature and broken beans, foreign matter; calculate as a percentage by weight.

Step 7Roast the sample

Roast light to medium, with the same roast profile for every sample.

Step 8Taste

Rest 8–24 hours, 5 cups per sample, taste blind. Fine Robusta or SCA scale.

Step 9Lab if needed

Caffeine and 16-OMC by HPLC or NMR; DNA to identify the line.

Step 10Keep a sample

Keep part of the sample as a reference until the lot is used up, and fill in the form.
